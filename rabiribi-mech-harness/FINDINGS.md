# Bounds map for in-memory save/restore (mechanical harness)

The aim here is not "it never fails". It is to *know the bounds*: to take every
failure mode the Rabi-Ribi savestate documentation names, provoke it on demand,
show the mitigation that steps around it, and then iterate until we can say with
numbers that a save point can be created and put back in RAM as many times over
as we like, in the situations we have mapped. Not-knowing is the enemy; a
reproducible fault next to a working mitigation is the win.

Everything below is produced by `rr_mech_harness.c`, a self-contained model that
does **not** link the real `savestate.c` and does **not** need the game. It
stands up a small world with the ownership shapes that matter, runs its own byte
snapshot/restore over one pinned 4 MB arena, and for each boundary runs the
pitfall (mitigation OFF, expected to be detected) against the mitigation (ON,
expected clean). Numbers below are from `rr_mech_harness32.exe` (PE32) under
Wine 9.0; reproduced at 1, 6, 8 and 12 workers.

## How to read this

Each boundary is one of the three inconsistency classes from
`restore_invariants.md`, or a capture-time or device-time seam. For each we
state: what it is, how the harness provokes it, how the harness detects it, and
the mitigation. The measured result is `pitfall detected / mitigation clean`.

The three classes are generated by the snapshot boundary, and the list is
closed:

- **Class A - outward straddle.** Something *inside* the snapshot refers to
  something *outside* it. The reference is rewound to its save-time value; the
  target moved on.
- **Class B - inward straddle.** Something *outside* the snapshot refers to
  something *inside* it. The reference stays; the target is rewound underneath
  it.
- **Class C - captured mid-mutation.** Both halves are inside the snapshot and
  the structure was inconsistent at the instant of capture. No boundary can be
  moved to fix this; the only repair is not to take the picture at that moment.

## The bounds

### Class C - mid-mutation capture

- **Pitfall.** Copy the arena while the writer threads run. A node whose fields
  are written but whose checksum is not yet written is photographed torn.
- **Detection.** A writer-maintained checksum plus the node's own callback
  answer; a torn node fails both. (Struct-internal, never layout-guessed:
  identity is proven by *calling* the node's callback.)
- **Mitigation.** Quiesce the writers at a cooperative safe-point barrier before
  the copy. Writers only park between whole mutations, so the snapshot never
  lands inside a mutation, however wide the mutation window is.
- **Measured.** pitfall **200/200 detected** · mitigation **0/200**.

### Capture method - SuspendThread vs a cooperative barrier

This is the seam the docs flag as behaving differently off real Windows.

- **Pitfall.** Take the real engine's route: `SuspendThread` every writer and
  copy. Suspension lands at an arbitrary instruction, so a writer frozen
  mid-update is photographed torn - which is exactly why the real engine needs a
  settle pass, and exactly the pass its own notes say is skipped under Wine.
- **Detection.** Same identity oracle.
- **Mitigation.** Do not freeze writers at arbitrary points; let them reach a
  safe point and park. A cooperative barrier cannot photograph a torn node.
- **Measured.** pitfall **200/200 detected** · mitigation **0/200**.
- **Note.** This is the strongest practical finding for the port: *the capture
  method, not the copy, is the fragile part under Wine.* A safe-point barrier
  sidesteps the whole SuspendThread + settle-loop problem the real engine wrestles
  with.

### Class A - outward straddle (record-and-replay)

Models `ntdll!RtlpDynamicFunctionTable` / a COM pointer into wrapper memory: a
list head lives outside the snapshot, its entries inside.

- **Pitfall.** A snapshot-side node holds an index into a present-side table.
  Between save and restore the present side moves that table entry on. After the
  rewind the node's index is back to its save value but the target has changed.
- **Detection.** The node's expected token no longer matches the present table.
- **Mitigation.** Record the depended-on present value at save and replay it at
  restore - the same shape as the engine's unwind-table fix.
- **Measured.** pitfall **200/200 detected** · mitigation **0/200**.

### Class B - inward straddle (exclude / re-pin)

Models the eight blocks on the game heap held by system-thread TEBs: present
holders of rewound memory.

- **Pitfall.** A present thread mutates a block that is inside the snapshot and
  expects the mutation to stand. The rewind reverts the block under it.
- **Detection.** The present holder's recorded value is gone after the restore.
- **Mitigation.** Exclude the live-held block from the rewind (lift it out
  before the copy, put it back after) - object-retirement / re-pin.
- **Measured.** pitfall **200/200 detected** · mitigation **0/200**.

### Audio cursor split (reseat)

Models the DirectSound experiment in `ds_harness.c`.

- **Pitfall.** The write cursor is an ordinary rewound value; the hardware play
  cursor keeps advancing across the rewind. Afterwards the write pointer is
  behind the play cursor and the streaming length is negative - a glitch.
- **Detection.** A nonzero gap between the rewound write cursor and the live play
  cursor after the restore.
- **Mitigation.** Reseat the write cursor to the play cursor on restore.
- **Measured.** pitfall **200/200 detected** · mitigation **0/200**.

### Always-on invariants

Checked on every restore, not just in their own arm:

- **Determinism.** A pinned arena and a reproducible RNG/clock stream; the next
  16 RNG draws recorded at save must replay identically after the rewind.
  Negative control C3 perturbs the stream and is caught.
- **Heap metadata self-consistency.** The arena allocator's free list is walked
  the way `HeapValidate` walks a heap: free + used must equal capacity, no cycle,
  no in-use node on the free list. (Kept off the live Win32 heap on purpose - the
  docs say rewinding a held Wine heap hangs.)
- **Thread set.** The saved worker roster is diffed against the live one after
  each restore (invariant #4): fresh/gone accounting, 0/0 in this rig.

## The instrument is shown to bite

Following `restore_invariants.md` rule two - a check that has never failed is not
yet a check - three negative controls run first and must each fire:

- **C1** torn/mid-mutation capture → **DETECTED**
- **C2** a corrupted restored node → **DETECTED**
- **C3** a broken determinism stream → **DETECTED**

## Confidence run (soak)

With every mapped mitigation engaged, and Class A + Class B stressors active, the
soak ran **5000 save/restore cycles, 5000 restores, 0 failures**
(`id=0 det=0 heap=0 A=0 B=0 cursor=0`), reproduced at 1/6/8/12 workers.

## The blocker, and the fix: never commit a poisoned capture

A bad capture is **not self-healing**. A save taken while the world is
inconsistent writes that damage into the slot, and every later restore
reproduces it. A poisoned slot is permanent, not a transient you can restore
away from. This is the current blocker, and the evidence points at *our capture
handling*, not Wine or Windows: the copy is fine; the instant we choose to copy
is the problem.

The trivial win - a cooperative safe-point barrier - is **not portable**: the
game's and Mono's threads are not ours to park. So the fix has to work with what
the engine actually has (`SuspendThread`). The harness models capture as a
strategy and measures the only number that matters, *poisoned* (a committed
snapshot that then restores wrong):

| strategy | committed | refused | poisoned | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RACE (copy while running) | 200 | 0 | 200 | POISONS |
| SUSPEND (engine today, no settle) | 200 | 0 | ~199 | POISONS |
| SUSPEND+SETTLE (retry until no writer in a known hot region) | 200 | 0 | 0 | safe\* |
| **SUSPEND+VERIFY** (copy to scratch, verify, commit only if clean, else retry/refuse) | 200 | 0 | **0** | **safe** |
| BARRIER (cooperative safe point) | 200 | 0 | 0 | safe (not portable) |
| SUSPEND+VERIFY, retry budget = 2 | 84 | 116 | **0** | safe (refused, not corrupt) |

Soak with **SUSPEND+VERIFY** and Class A+B stressors active:
**4000 save/restore cycles, 4000 committed, 0 refused, 0 poisoned**
(`id=0 det=0 heap=0 A=0 B=0 cursor=0`), ~2.3 retries per save.

\* SETTLE shows 0 here, but its safety is only as good as its list of hot
regions - it trusts that "no thread is in a region I know about" means
consistent. VERIFY makes no such assumption: it checks the actual captured bytes
(identity through each object's own callback + checksum, plus a heap-metadata
walk) and commits only what is proven consistent. That is why VERIFY is the
recommendation.

### The recommended change to the real engine

In the save path, after the suspend-and-copy, **run the same consistency oracles
on the freshly captured snapshot that the restore path already runs, and commit
the snapshot only if it passes; otherwise resume, back off, and retry, and after
a bounded number of tries refuse the save.** The engine already validates heaps
before a save and fails closed on the restore side; this simply extends that
discipline to *committing* a capture. Concretely:

1. Do not skip the consistency gate under Wine. The whole-module settle loop is
   what gets skipped there; a post-capture verify is cheap (it is the checks
   already written for restore) and does not depend on enumerating hot regions.
2. Capture into scratch, resume the world immediately (minimal suspend time),
   then verify the scratch offline; only swap it into the live slot if clean.
3. On repeated failure, **refuse the save** and keep the last good slot. A
   refused save is a retry the caller can surface; a poisoned slot is a silent
   corruption discovered frames later. The whole point of the bounds map is to
   trade the second for the first.

The cost is retries (measured ~2-3 per save at this contention), which is the
same cost the settle loop pays - but bounded, and paid only to *avoid* poisoning
rather than to guess at safe instants.

## What this does and does not establish

It establishes that the *mechanics* of create-a-save-point / restore-in-RAM
are sound for these ownership shapes, that each named failure mode is real and
reproducible, and that each has a mitigation that holds over thousands of
iterations. It does **not** reproduce the full engine's live-heap rewind,
stack/context rewind, or GPU/audio device ownership - those are Windows-internal
and are what the real `savestate.c` and its Wine notes wrestle with. The value
here is the map: the bounds are named, each is provokable on command, and the
way around each is demonstrated.
